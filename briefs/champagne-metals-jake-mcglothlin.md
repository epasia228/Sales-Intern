# Account Brief — Champagne Metals
**Contact:** Jake McGlothlin — VP of Sales & Marketing
**Signal:** Acquisition of Erickson Metals (December 2024) — added extrusions/shapes product line, new sales team, new geographies
**Brief Date:** March 2026

---

### 1. Company Snapshot

Champagne Metals is a privately held aluminum service center based in Glenpool, Oklahoma — $35–38M revenue, ~120 employees across 4 processing facilities (OK, IN, MO, WV) plus stocking locations in Canada and Mexico. They are in an active growth phase: one acquisition closed in December 2024, a major facility expansion underway in Indiana, a new stocking location opened in Missouri, and a dedicated Canada sales hire just made. They self-describe as "America's fastest growing aluminum service center" — and the growth is real, but the infrastructure supporting it is not publicly visible.

---

### 2. Contact Context

McGlothlin owns revenue generation across a direct B2B sales team serving repeat, high-volume manufacturers — Class 8 truck makers, trailer OEMs, automotive stampers, marine, and defense. He is measured on new account acquisition and retention of production customers who buy consistently and in volume. His pressure right now is practical: he is managing a larger team, a wider product catalog (sheet/coil + extrusions post-Erickson), new geographies, and a Canada expansion — almost certainly without a unified system tracking any of it.

---

### 3. Signal Interpretation

The Erickson Metals acquisition didn't just add product lines — it added a legacy sales team with its own customer relationships, quoting habits, and account history, none of which maps cleanly onto how Champagne's existing team works. Merging two sales motions across a family-owned, relationship-driven industrial business without a CRM means account ownership is ambiguous, pipeline visibility is whoever picks up the phone, and the Canada expansion will compound the problem immediately. The founder publicly flagged knowledge retention as a company risk — that's a CRM problem dressed up as a culture concern.

---

### 4. Core Hypothesis

Champagne Metals is running a multi-site, post-acquisition, geographically expanding direct sales operation on relationship memory and spreadsheets — and the Erickson integration is the moment where that approach starts costing real accounts.

---

### 5. Angle

When a company acquires a 50-year-old distributor, the hardest thing to integrate isn't the equipment or the inventory — it's the customer knowledge that lives in the heads of the Erickson sales team. Without a system, that knowledge either walks out the door when someone leaves or stays siloed indefinitely. For a business where production customers buy on relationship and consistency, that's not a data problem — it's a revenue retention risk. Curious whether the integration surfaced any gaps in how account history is being managed across the two teams.
