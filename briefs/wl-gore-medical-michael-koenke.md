# Account Brief — W.L. Gore Medical Products Division
**Contact:** Michael Koenke — Global Sales and Marketing Leader, Gore Medical Products Division
**Signal:** Conformal Medical acquisition closed February 2026 + open Global Sales Operations Leader role + known Salesforce adoption lag across 2,200 users in 49 countries
**Brief Date:** March 2026

---

### 1. Company Snapshot

Gore Medical is the medical devices division of W.L. Gore & Associates — a $5.3B privately held company with ~13,000 employees globally. The Medical division sells cardiovascular and surgical devices direct to hospitals and health systems across 90+ countries, with 50M+ devices implanted to date. They run Salesforce as enterprise CRM across the division, with over 2,200 users across 49 countries — a deployment that has outgrown the processes built around it.

---

### 2. Contact Context

Koenke owns global sales, marketing, commercial analytics, and operations for the Medical Products Division, and sits on the divisional executive leadership team. He is measured on commercial performance across a complex, multi-region business that sells through direct reps, IDN contracts, and GPO relationships simultaneously. His pressure right now is two-layered: integrate a freshly acquired company into an existing commercial infrastructure, while the Global Sales Operations Leader role — the function that governs that infrastructure — is either vacant or recently turned over.

---

### 3. Signal Interpretation

The Conformal Medical acquisition isn't just a pipeline addition — it's 60 people, a clinical-stage product with a distinct sales motion (investigational device, trial enrollment, hospital credentialing), and a new commercial team that needs to be onboarded into a Salesforce org that Gore already acknowledged has adoption and process consistency problems. Meanwhile, the open Global Sales Operations Leader posting explicitly calls out CRM/Salesforce governance, process standardization, and digital tool evolution as core responsibilities. A vacancy in that role during an acquisition integration is a material gap — not a minor one.

---

### 4. Core Hypothesis

Gore's Salesforce org was built division by division, which means the data model, process logic, and adoption standards vary across segments — and the Conformal integration will surface those inconsistencies faster than any internal cleanup initiative would have.

---

### 5. Angle

Most medtech companies with 2,000+ Salesforce users across 50 countries have a version of the same problem: the CRM reflects how the business was organized when it was implemented, not how it actually runs today. When that org absorbs a newly acquired commercial team — even a small one — the gaps in process consistency and data ownership become visible immediately, usually in the first forecasting cycle post-close. Curious whether the Conformal integration is exposing anything in how the commercial infrastructure is set up today.
